# NBACareerPredictorML

This a machine learning project that predicts the careers of the new NBA rookies to see if they'll become a rotational, starter, all-star or superstar. To view the results go into the predictions folder, and select the prospect_predictions2.csv. It is the most accurate of the models in the folders. If you want to see my analysis of the data, you will be able to find it in the txt file. 

Interesting Insights of the results of the machine learning model:
1. Cameron Boozer is the top player of the draft.
   If you asked any nba scouts going into the draft, they would say that AJ Dybantsa and Darryn Peterson are the best players available in the entire draft. However, Cameron Boozer scored better than any other player consistently in every prediction.

2. The Atlanta Hawks found some diamonds in the rough.
   Kingston Flemings consistently scored higher than other guards that were selected before him, like Keaton Wagler, Darius Acuff Jr., and Mikel Brown Jr. He was predicted most to be an all-star in this draft that was drafted outside the top four. (AJ Dybantsa, Darryn Peterson, Cameron Boozer, and Caleb Wilson) Zuby Ejiofor was considered to be an All-star favorite by the model, which is surprising considering he was drafted 23rd to the Atlanta Hawks. The only Atlanta Hawk player that wasn't favored by the model was Henri Veesar. The model considered him to be at best a rotational player.

3. Other players that did surprising well in the model
   Tarris Reed Jr., Jaden Bradley, and Koa Peat were predicted to have good careers compared to the rest of their draft class. While Jaden Bradley hasn't proved much from summer league, he has still shown potential to be a quality starter in the league. On the other hand, Tarris Reed Jr. and Koa Peat have been showing strong potential to becoming dominant players in the league.

Conclusion:
Sports Analytics don't mean everything when it comes to professional sports. If that was the case, then majority of the front offices would be filled with analytics experts instead of traditional scouts. The best way to incorporate these findings would be to cooperate with everyone in the front office and communicate clearly what you've found out from the data. It's hard to predict the future with only numbers, because there are cases where there are outliers. For example, when Jaylen Brown was in trade conversations this offseason, the analytics department decided that he was the 8th best player on the Celtics. Anyone with common basketball knowledge would understand that Jaylen Brown is worth more than that. Even though there are cases like these, I believe that Sports Analytics and Machine Learning are the future to help front office decisions in the future.
